For the first question, verification basically means checking AI’s work to make sure that it
is actually correct and can be trusted. So, just because AI gives you an answer really
quickly doesn’t mean that the answer is right. Someone still has to look at it and check
the information and make sure there aren’t any mistakes. The authors are basically
saying that AI can produce things faster and faster, but humans still have to spend time
verifying those things.
For the second question, I think the gap is a problem because AI can produce an answer
really quickly, but checking whether that answer is actually good can take a lot longer.
For example, AI could easily create a business strategy or marketing plan in a few
seconds, but it would be harder to know if that strategy would actually work in the real
world. You might not know if it was a good strategy until months or even years later. So
the problem is that AI can create a lot more work than people can realistically check.
For the third question, I’m interested in going into business management, and eventually
I would like to own my own business. One thing a beginner in business might do to gain
experience is helping with things like creating reports, analyzing information, doing
research, or helping make business decisions. If AI takes over some of those beginner
tasks, future professionals might miss out on actually learning how businesses work by
doing those things themselves. They might know how to use AI, but not fully understand
how to tell if the AI is making a good decision.
One way to build that experience instead would be through internships, working directly
with experienced managers, or doing projects where you actually have to make decisions
and then see what happens. I think that would still give people the experience they
normally would have gotten from those beginner jobs.
